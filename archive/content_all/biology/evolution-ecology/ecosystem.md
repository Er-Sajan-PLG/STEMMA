  A biological community of interacting organisms (biotic factors) together with their non-living physical and
  chemical environment (abiotic factors), functioning as an integrated ecological system.
symbol: null

equation: null
examples:
  - "A pond, a forest and a coral reef are ecosystems."
  - "Decomposers break down dead matter and recycle nutrients in a compost heap."
key_experiments:
  - "Build a sealed bottle biome (mini ecosystem) or survey a local pond or lawn with a quadrat."
common_misconceptions:
  - Ecosystems are static and unchanging when undisturbed (ecosystems experience continuous dynamic fluctuations and succession).
  - Decomposers are unimportant compared to predators (decomposers recycle essential chemical nutrients without which ecosystems collapse).
learning_objectives:
  - Distinguish biotic components (producers, consumers, decomposers) from abiotic components (temperature, light, water, nutrients).
  - Trace energy flow (one-way) and nutrient cycling (recycled) through ecosystems.
  - Analyze how changes in biotic or abiotic factors affect ecosystem stability.
real_world_applications:
  - Wildlife habitat conservation and restoration ecology.
  - Ecosystem service evaluation (water purification, pollination, flood control).

## Notes

Ecosystem stability depends on biodiversity and nutrient cycling between abiotic and biotic pools.
