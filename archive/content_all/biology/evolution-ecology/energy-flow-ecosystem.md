definition: >-
  The one-way, non-cyclical movement of energy through an ecosystem, entering as sunlight captured by
  producers, passing through successive trophic levels as chemical energy, and being progressively lost to
  the environment as heat through respiration at each transfer.

  - "Only about 10% of energy passes between trophic levels (the '10% law'), limiting the number of levels an ecosystem can support."

  - "Lindeman's trophic-dynamic studies of a lake ecosystem quantified the efficiency of energy transfer between trophic levels."
common_misconceptions:
  - Energy is recycled within an ecosystem (matter cycles, but energy flows one way and is lost as heat).
  - All energy captured by photosynthesis is available to herbivores (a large fraction is used in plant respiration and lost).

  - Distinguish the flow of energy from the cycling of matter.

## Notes

Energy flow governs ecosystem structure: because transfer is inefficient, ecosystems support only a limited
number of trophic levels.
