name: pH Scale
domain: chemistry
status: draft
definition: >-
  A logarithmic scale measuring the acidity or alkalinity of an aqueous solution, defined as the negative
  base-10 logarithm of the hydrogen ion concentration (pH = -log₁₀[H⁺]), ranging from 0 (highly acidic) to 14 (highly alkaline), with 7 being neutral.
symbol: pH
unit: null
equation: pH = -log₁₀[H⁺]
examples:
  - "Lemon juice is about pH 2, pure water pH 7, and soap pH 9-10."
  - "Soil pH decides which crops grow well in a region."
key_experiments:
  - "Use universal indicator against a colour chart to measure the pH of a range of household solutions."
common_misconceptions:
  - "A change of 1 pH unit represents a 1-fold change in acidity (pH is logarithmic: a 1-unit change is a 10-fold change in H⁺ concentration)."
  - Neutral pH is always 7 regardless of temperature (neutral pH equals 7 at 25 °C, but changes slightly with temperature).
learning_objectives:
  - Interpret values on the 0–14 pH scale (acidic < 7, neutral = 7, basic > 7).
  - Use indicators (litmus, universal indicator, pH meters) to estimate solution pH.
  - Calculate H⁺ concentration from pH and vice versa.
real_world_applications:
  - Soil testing for agricultural crop suitability.
  - Swimming pool and aquarium water quality maintenance.
provenance:
  ai_drafted: true
  source_kind: standards-or-specification
  source: IUPAC Quantities, Units and Symbols in Physical Chemistry

## Notes

pH measurement governs chemical kinetics, enzyme activity, and environmental quality.
