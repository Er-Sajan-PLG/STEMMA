definition: >-
  An ionic compound formed when the hydrogen ion of an acid is replaced by a metal ion
  (or ammonium ion), typically produced by neutralization of an acid with a base.
  Common table salt is sodium chloride, NaCl.
symbol: null

examples:
  - "Table salt is sodium chloride; ammonium nitrate is a salt used in fertilizers."
  - "Road salt lowers the freezing point of water for de-icing."
key_experiments:
  - "Neutralize hydrochloric acid with sodium hydroxide and evaporate to obtain sodium chloride crystals."
common_misconceptions:
  - Salt means only sodium chloride (a salt is any ionic compound formed from acid-base chemistry; NaCl is one example).
  - All salts are safe to eat (many salts such as ammonium nitrate or heavy-metal salts are toxic or hazardous).
  - Salts are always neutral (salts of weak acids or weak bases can form acidic or basic solutions).

  - Define a salt and describe how it forms from an acid and a base.

  - Predict the salt produced by a given acid-base pair.

  - Table salt and food preservation.
  - Fertilizers (ammonium sulfate, potassium nitrate).
  - De-icing roads (sodium chloride lowers the freezing point).

## Notes

The salt concept ties the acids-bases unit together: naming and predicting salts is the
practical outcome of understanding neutralization reactions.
