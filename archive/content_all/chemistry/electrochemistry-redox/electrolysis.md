name: Electrolysis
domain: chemistry
status: draft
definition: >-
  The decomposition of a compound into its elements or simpler substances by passing an electric
  current through an electrolyte. Electrolysis is driven by an external voltage and is the key
  process in an electrolytic cell, enabling nonspontaneous redox reactions.
symbol: null
unit: null
equation: null
examples:
  - "Electrolysis of molten sodium chloride yields sodium metal and chlorine gas."
  - "Electrolysis of water produces hydrogen and oxygen gases."
key_experiments:
  - "Electrolyse acidified water using inert electrodes and collect the hydrogen and oxygen gases."
common_misconceptions:
  - An electrolyte conducts electrons through its bulk (ions carry charge in the electrolyte).
  - The same products form regardless of electrode material (electrode material can participate).
learning_objectives:
  - Explain electrolysis in terms of migration of ions toward electrodes.
  - Predict products of electrolysis for molten and aqueous electrolytes.
  - Relate the amount of substance produced to the charge passed (Faraday's laws).
real_world_applications:
  - Production of aluminium, chlorine, and hydrogen.
  - Electroplating of metals and rechargeable battery operation.
