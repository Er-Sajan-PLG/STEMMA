type: concept
name: Oxidation-Reduction
domain: chemistry
status: draft
definition: >-
  A class of chemical reactions involving the transfer of electrons between species, in which
  oxidation is the loss of electrons and reduction is the gain of electrons. Oxidation and reduction
  always occur together, with one species being oxidized and another reduced.
symbol: null
unit: null
equation: "X → Xⁿ⁺ + ne⁻  (oxidation);  Y + ne⁻ → Yⁿ⁻  (reduction)"
examples:
  - "Iron rusting, 4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃, is an oxidation-reduction reaction."
  - "Reaction of zinc metal with copper(II) ions transfers electrons when zinc is oxidized."
key_experiments:
  - "Dip a zinc strip into copper sulfate solution and observe copper plating while zinc dissolves."
common_misconceptions:
  - Oxidation always involves oxygen (oxidation is defined as loss of electrons, oxygen not required).
  - A reaction can be only oxidation or only reduction (the two always occur together).
learning_objectives:
  - Assign oxidation and reduction as electron loss and gain.
  - Identify oxidizing and reducing agents in a reaction.
  - Balance oxidation-reduction reactions by the half-reaction method.
real_world_applications:
  - Batteries, corrosion of metals, and biological respiration.
  - Bleaching, electroplating, and metallurgical extraction.
