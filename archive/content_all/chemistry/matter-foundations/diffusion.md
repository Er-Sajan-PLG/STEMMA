  The net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of
  lower concentration, driven by the random thermal motion of particles, until
  concentrations equalize.

  - "A drop of ink spreads through water on its own."

  - "Drop potassium permanganate into hot and cold water: diffusion is faster at higher temperature."
common_misconceptions:
  - Diffusion happens only in gases (diffusion occurs in gases, liquids, and across solutions).
  - Particles move because they are pushed toward empty space (particles move randomly; net flow arises from statistical imbalance, not repulsion).
  - Higher temperature slows diffusion (higher temperature increases particle kinetic energy and speeds diffusion).

  - Explain diffusion in terms of random particle motion.
  - Describe factors that affect the rate of diffusion (temperature, particle size, concentration gradient).
  - Distinguish diffusion from osmosis.

## Notes

Diffusion is the same particle-kinetic idea that underpins temperature and the random
motion of matter. It is also the mechanism by which many biological transports occur.
