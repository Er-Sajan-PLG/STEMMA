name: Carboxylic Acid
domain: chemistry
status: draft
definition: >-
  An organic compound containing a carboxyl functional group (—COOH), a carbonyl group bonded to a
  hydroxyl group. Carboxylic acids are weak acids that donate a proton from the hydroxyl group to
  form carboxylate anions.
symbol: null
unit: null
equation: "RCOOH ⇌ RCOO⁻ + H⁺  (generic ionization)"
examples:
  - "Ethanoic acid (CH₃COOH, vinegar) and citric acid in citrus fruit are carboxylic acids."
  - "Fatty acids contain long hydrocarbon chains ending in a carboxyl group."
key_experiments:
  - "Add a carboxylic acid to sodium carbonate and test the carbon dioxide evolved."
common_misconceptions:
  - Carboxylic acids are strong acids (they are typically weak acids that ionize only partially).
  - The carboxyl group alone determines intensity of acidity (substituents affect acid strength).
learning_objectives:
  - Identify the carboxyl group and write structural formulas of simple carboxylic acids.
  - Describe the acidic character and formation of carboxylate salts.
  - Explain esterification of carboxylic acids with alcohols.
real_world_applications:
  - Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) and other pharmaceuticals.
  - Preservatives and flavorings; soaps derived from fatty acids.
