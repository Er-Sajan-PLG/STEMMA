name: Polymer

definition: >-
  A large molecule built from many repeating monomer units joined by covalent bonds. Polymers may be
  natural, such as proteins and cellulose, or synthetic, such as polyethylene and nylon, formed by
  addition or condensation polymerization.
symbol: null
unit: null

examples:
  - "Polyethylene is formed by addition polymerization of ethene monomers."
  - "Nylon-66 and polyesters are produced by condensation polymerization."
key_experiments:
  - "Synthesize a nylon rope from interfacial polymerization and draw it from the interface."
common_misconceptions:
  - All polymers are synthetic plastics (many, like cellulose and proteins, are natural).
  - A polymer is a single fixed molecule (samples have a distribution of chain lengths).
learning_objectives:
  - Distinguish monomers, polymers, and the degree of polymerization.
  - Compare addition and condensation polymerization.
  - Relate polymer structure to physical properties such as flexibility and strength.
real_world_applications:
  - Plastics, fibers, and coatings in packaging, textiles, and construction.
  - Biopolymers in medicine and biodegradable materials.
