definition: >-
  A rapid chemical reaction between a substance and an oxidant (usually oxygen) that
  releases heat and light. Complete combustion of hydrocarbons produces carbon dioxide
  and water; incomplete combustion produces carbon monoxide and soot.
symbol: null

examples:
  - "Gas stoves, car engines and campfires run on combustion of fuel."
  - "Incomplete combustion makes soot and dangerous carbon monoxide."
key_experiments:
  - "Burn a small candle in a jar: observe moisture and test the gas with limewater to show CO₂ from complete combustion."
common_misconceptions:
  - Combustion can occur without oxygen (combustion requires an oxidant, normally oxygen in air).
  - Fire and combustion are the same thing (fire is the visible, observable event of combustion, not the reaction itself).
  - Complete combustion is always achieved with plenty of fuel (complete combustion needs sufficient oxygen, not more fuel).
learning_objectives:
  - Describe combustion as an exothermic oxidation reaction.
  - Distinguish complete from incomplete combustion and their products.
  - Relate combustion to everyday fuels and their environmental effects.

  - Burning fossil fuels for energy and transport.
  - Cooking and heating with gas burners.

## Notes

Combustion is the single most important energy-releasing reaction for human civilization,
and the clearest bridge between chemistry and the study of energy and the environment.
