type: concept
name: Arithmetic Sequence
domain: mathematics
status: draft
definition: >-
  A sequence in which each term differs from the previous one by a constant amount called the
  common difference d, so aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d. The terms grow or decrease linearly, and the sum of
  the first n terms can be written compactly as Sₙ = n/2 (a₁ + aₙ).
symbol: aₙ
unit: null
equation: "aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d   |   Sₙ = n/2 (a₁ + aₙ)"
examples:
  - "3, 7, 11, 15, … has common difference 4"
  - "aₙ = 2n − 1 gives the odd numbers 1, 3, 5, 7, …"
  - "50, 45, 40, … has common difference −5"
  - "The sequence 5, 8, 11 has sum S₃ = 24"
common_misconceptions:
  - "Arithmetic and geometric sequences are the same (arithmetic adds a constant; geometric multiplies by a constant)"
  - "The common difference must be positive (it may be zero or negative)"
learning_objectives:
  - Identify the common difference of an arithmetic sequence
  - Write the general term aₙ from the first term and difference
  - Compute the sum of an arithmetic series
  - Detect arithmetic patterns in applied contexts
real_world_applications:
  - "Steady slopes: costs, savings, and distances that increase by a fixed step"
  - Scheduling and stacking arrangements with uniform increments
key_experiments:
  - "Stack identical objects one row atop another to generate linear-step counts"
