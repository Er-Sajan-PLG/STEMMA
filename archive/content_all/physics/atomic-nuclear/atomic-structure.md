definition: >-
  The structure of an atom as a dense, positively charged nucleus (containing protons and neutrons)
  surrounded by negatively charged electrons in orbitals. The number of protons determines the element.

unit: null
equation: null
examples:
  - "A carbon-12 atom has 6 protons, 6 neutrons and 6 electrons."
  - "An ion forms when an atom gains or loses electrons."
key_experiments:
  - "Rutherford gold-foil model discussion/simulation: most alpha particles pass straight through a thin foil."
common_misconceptions:
  - Electrons orbit the nucleus in fixed paths like planets (electrons exist in probability clouds/orbitals).
  - The atom is mostly solid matter (the atom is mostly empty space).
  - All atoms of an element have the same number of neutrons (isotopes vary in neutron count).
learning_objectives:
  - Describe the atom as a nucleus surrounded by electrons.
  - Distinguish between protons, neutrons, and electrons.
  - Define atomic number and mass number.
  - Explain isotopes.
real_world_applications:
  - Nuclear energy.
  - Medical imaging (PET scans, MRI).
  - Radiometric dating.
  - Semiconductor technology.
