type: quantity
name: Nuclear Binding Energy
domain: physics
status: draft
definition: The energy required to separate a nucleus into its individual nucleons, equivalent
  to the mass defect through the mass-energy relation. It reflects the energy released when a
  nucleus forms from its constituents.
symbol: E_b
unit: joule (J) per nucleus; electronvolt (eV) per nucleon
equation: E_b = Δm·c², where Δm is the mass defect
examples:
  - "Iron-56 has among the highest binding energy per nucleon, making it unusually stable."
  - "Splitting heavy nuclei or fusing light nuclei releases energy as they move toward higher binding per nucleon."
key_experiments:
  - "Mass spectrometers measure nuclide masses; comparing them to constituent nucleons yields the mass defect."
common_misconceptions:
  - "Binding energy is not energy stored inside the nucleus as if it were fuel; it is energy that must be supplied to break it apart."
  - "A bound nucleus has less mass, not more, than its separated constituents."
learning_objectives:
  - "Relate binding energy to mass defect via E = Δm·c²."
  - "Interpret the binding-energy-per-nucleon curve to explain fission and fusion energy release."
real_world_applications:
  - "Explaining why both nuclear fission and fusion release energy."
  - "Assessing nuclide stability in reactor and weapon design."
