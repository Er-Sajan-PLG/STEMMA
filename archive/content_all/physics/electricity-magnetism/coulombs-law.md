name: Coulomb's Law
domain: physics
status: draft
definition: >-
  The electrostatic force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of
  their magnitudes and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.
symbol: null
unit: null
equation: F = k·|q₁·q₂| / r² (k ≈ 9×10⁹ N·m²/C²)
real_world_applications:
  - "Static cling and electrostatic painting rely on attraction between charges."
  - "Understanding lightning and electrostatic precipitators that clean industrial smoke."
examples:
  - "A charged balloon is pushed away by a like-charged one."
  - "Static cling holds a charged balloon to a wall."
key_experiments:
  - "Pith balls: observe repulsion between like charges and stronger force at smaller separations (inverse-square behaviour)."
common_misconceptions:
  - Coulomb's law applies to charges of any size (it is exact for point charges).
  - Force between charges depends on the medium (it does — the constant k changes).
  - Coulomb's law is the same as Newton's gravitation (both are inverse-square but differ in detail).
learning_objectives:
  - State Coulomb's law.
  - Calculate electrostatic force between two charges.
  - Compare electrostatic and gravitational forces.
  - Explain the inverse-square nature of electrostatic force.

  note: "Established experimentally using a torsion balance. An inverse-square law for electrostatics had also been suggested earlier by Joseph Priestley (1767) and Henry Cavendish, so the 'first' formulation is contested; Coulomb provided the quantitative experimental basis."
