definition: >-
  The phenomenon by which materials exert attractive or repulsive forces on other materials.
  Magnetism is intimately connected to electricity — moving charges produce magnetic fields,
  and changing magnetic fields produce electric currents.

key_experiments:
  - "Magnetize a steel pin by stroking and observe north-south attraction and repulsion."
examples:
  - "A fridge magnet holds notes; a compass aligns with Earth's magnetic field."
  - "Speaker magnets drive the cone to produce sound."
common_misconceptions:
  - All metals are magnetic (only iron, nickel, cobalt, and some alloys are ferromagnetic).
  - Magnets attract all metals (only magnetic materials).
  - Magnetic poles can exist independently (breaking a magnet produces two smaller magnets, each with both poles).
learning_objectives:
  - Describe magnetic materials and their properties.
  - Identify magnetic poles and their interactions.
  - Map magnetic field lines around a bar magnet.
  - Distinguish between permanent magnets and electromagnets.
real_world_applications:
  - Compass navigation.
  - Electric motors and generators.
  - MRI (magnetic resonance imaging).
  - Magnetic storage (hard drives).
