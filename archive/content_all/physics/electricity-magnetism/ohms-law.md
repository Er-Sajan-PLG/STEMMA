id: stemma:phys.ohms-law
type: law
name: Ohm's Law
domain: physics
status: draft
definition: The current through a conductor between two points is directly proportional
  to the voltage across the two points, provided the temperature remains constant.
  The constant of proportionality is the resistance.
symbol: null
unit: null
equation: V = I·R
examples:
- Doubling the voltage across a fixed resistor doubles the current.
- Adding a series resistor dims a lamp — less current flows.
key_experiments:
- Vary voltage across a resistor, record current, and plot I against V; the slope
  is 1/R.
- Measuring V and I for a fixed resistor to verify Ohm's law.
common_misconceptions:
- Ohm's law applies to all conductors (only ohmic conductors show linear V-I relationship).
- Resistance depends on voltage and current (for ohmic conductors, resistance is constant).
- Ohm's law is always true (it breaks down at very high currents or for non-ohmic
  materials).
learning_objectives:
- State Ohm's law in words and mathematically.
- Calculate voltage, current, or resistance using V = IR.
- Plot and interpret V-I graphs for ohmic and non-ohmic conductors.
- Distinguish between ohmic and non-ohmic conductors.
real_world_applications:
- Circuit design.
- Fuse and breaker sizing.
- Sensor design (thermistors, LDRs).
