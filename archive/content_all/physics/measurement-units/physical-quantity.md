name: Physical Quantity
domain: physics
status: draft
definition: A property of a phenomenon, body, or substance that can be quantified
  — that is, expressed as a number and a reference (unit). Physical quantities describe
  the physical world in measurable terms, enabling comparison, calculation, and prediction.
symbol: null
unit: null
equation: null
examples:
- Length, mass, time, temperature and electric current are base physical quantities.
- Speed is a derived quantity built from length and time.
key_experiments:
- Classify laboratory instruments by the quantity they measure; build a base-vs-derived
  quantity table.
- Comparing measured values with accepted values to understand accuracy.
common_misconceptions:
- Physical quantities are the same as the objects or substances they describe.
- Every property of an object is a physical quantity (only quantifiable properties
  are).
learning_objectives:
- Distinguish between physical and non-physical properties.
- Classify quantities as scalar or vector.
- Identify the physical quantities relevant to a given phenomenon.
real_world_applications:
- Engineering design and safety calculations.
- Scientific measurement and data collection.
- Navigation, construction, and manufacturing.

## Notes

Curriculum-agnostic: this defines what it means for a property to be a physical quantity. It does not enumerate specific quantities (those are separate entities). All measurable physics begins here.
