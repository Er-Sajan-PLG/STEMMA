name: Buoyancy
domain: physics
status: draft
definition: >-
  The upward force exerted by a fluid on an object immersed in it. This force equals the weight
  of the fluid displaced by the object (Archimedes' principle).
symbol: null
unit: newton (N)
equation: F_buoyant = ρ_fluid · V_displaced · g
examples:
  - "A ship floats because its average density is lower than water's."
  - "A rubber duck floats; a stone sinks."
common_misconceptions:
  - Floating objects experience no buoyant force (they do — it equals their weight).
  - Only light objects float (shape matters — steel ships float).
  - Buoyancy depends on the object's mass (it depends on the fluid's density and displaced volume).
learning_objectives:
  - Explain buoyancy as an upward force from a fluid.
  - State Archimedes' principle.
  - Calculate buoyant force.
  - Explain why objects float or sink.
real_world_applications:
  - Ships and submarines.
  - Hot air balloons.
  - Life jackets.
  - Hydrometers.
key_experiments:
  - "Measuring buoyant force on an object in water."
  - "Verifying Archimedes' principle."
