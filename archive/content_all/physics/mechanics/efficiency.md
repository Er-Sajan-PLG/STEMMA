type: concept
name: Efficiency
domain: physics
status: draft
definition: >-
  The ratio of useful energy output to total energy input, expressed as a percentage. No real
  machine can be 100% efficient because some energy is always dissipated (usually as heat).
symbol: η (eta)
unit: null (dimensionless, or %)
equation: η = (E_useful_out / E_total_in) × 100%
examples:
  - "A 20%-efficient solar panel turns 80% of incoming light to heat; an LED bulb (≈90%) far beats a filament bulb (≈5%)."
  - "Pulley systems reduce effort but lose energy to friction."
key_experiments:
  - "Measure electrical energy in and useful mechanical energy out of a small motor or model crane; compute efficiency."
common_misconceptions:
  - Efficiency can exceed 100% (this would violate conservation of energy).
  - Inefficient machines destroy energy (energy is conserved; it just becomes less useful).
  - All energy losses can be eliminated (second law of thermodynamics prevents this).
learning_objectives:
  - Define efficiency as the ratio of useful output to total input.
  - Calculate efficiency in energy conversion processes.
  - Explain why efficiency is always less than 100%.
  - Suggest ways to improve efficiency in real systems.
real_world_applications:
  - Engine design (improving fuel efficiency).
  - Electrical appliances (energy ratings).
  - Building insulation.
  - Renewable energy systems.
