name: Kinetic Energy
domain: physics

definition: >-
  The energy possessed by an object due to its motion. The kinetic energy of an object depends on
  its mass and the square of its speed.
symbol: KE or K
unit: joule (J)
equation: KE = ½·m·v²
examples:
  - "A moving truck has far more kinetic energy than a bicycle at the same speed."
  - "Wind turns turbine blades — the air's kinetic energy becomes electricity."
key_experiments:
  - "Ramp-and-block: objects released from higher ramps (more KE = ½mv²) push a block farther."
common_misconceptions:
  - Kinetic energy is directly proportional to speed (it depends on speed squared — doubling
    speed quadruples kinetic energy).
  - Kinetic energy can be negative (kinetic energy is always non-negative).
  - Heavier objects always have more kinetic energy (at the same speed, yes; at different speeds, not necessarily).
learning_objectives:
  - Define kinetic energy as energy of motion.
  - Calculate kinetic energy from mass and speed.
  - Explain why kinetic energy depends on speed squared.
  - Relate kinetic energy to work done on an object.
real_world_applications:
  - Vehicle braking distance (doubles with the square of speed).
  - Sports (a faster ball has much more energy).
  - Wind turbines (kinetic energy of wind converted to electricity).
  - Bullet penetration.

## Notes

Kinetic energy is the most intuitive form of mechanical energy. Its dependence on speed squared
has profound real-world consequences — braking distance, collision damage, and fuel consumption
all scale nonlinearly with speed.
