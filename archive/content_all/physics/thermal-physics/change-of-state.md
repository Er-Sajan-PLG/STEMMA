type: concept
name: Change of State
domain: physics
status: draft
definition: The transition of matter between solid, liquid, and gas phases. During
  a change of state, temperature remains constant as energy is used to break or form
  intermolecular bonds.
symbol: null
unit: null
equation: Q = m·L (L = latent heat)
examples:
- Ice melts to water at 0 °C; water boils to steam at 100 °C at 1 atm.
- Steam condenses on a cold window pane.
key_experiments:
- Heat ice through melting to boiling while plotting a temperature-time curve — watch
  the plateaus.
- Heating ice to water to steam and recording temperature over time.
common_misconceptions:
- Temperature rises during melting/boiling (it stays constant until the change is
  complete).
- Boiling only happens at 100°C (boiling point depends on pressure).
- Steam at 100°C is not dangerous (it contains large amounts of latent heat).
learning_objectives:
- Describe melting, freezing, boiling, condensation, sublimation, and deposition.
- Explain why temperature is constant during a change of state.
- Calculate energy required for changes of state using Q = mL.
- Interpret heating curves.
real_world_applications:
- Refrigeration and air conditioning.
- Cooking (boiling, steaming).
- Ice production.
- Distillation.
