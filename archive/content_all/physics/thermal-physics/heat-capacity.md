id: stemma:phys.heat-capacity
type: quantity
name: Heat Capacity
domain: physics
status: draft
definition: The amount of heat required to raise the temperature of an entire object by one
  unit of temperature. It depends on the object's size and material, unlike the intensive
  specific heat which is heat capacity per unit mass.
symbol: C
unit: joule per kelvin (J/K)
equation: C = Q/ΔT
examples:
  - "A large metal engine block has a higher heat capacity than a small one, so it warms more slowly."
  - "A full bottle of water resists temperature change longer than an empty one of similar surface area."
key_experiments:
  - "Supply a known quantity of heat to an object and measure the resulting temperature rise."
common_misconceptions:
  - "Heat capacity is an extensive property: it grows with the amount of material."
  - "Heat capacity is not the same as specific heat, which is normalized per unit mass."
learning_objectives:
  - "Distinguish heat capacity from specific heat capacity."
  - "Compute temperature change from supplied heat and heat capacity."
real_world_applications:
  - "Designing thermal reservoirs and engine cooling systems."
  - "Choosing building materials with favourable thermal inertia."
