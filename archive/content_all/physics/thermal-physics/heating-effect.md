name: Heating Effect of Electric Current
domain: physics
status: draft
definition: >-
  The phenomenon by which electrical energy is converted to thermal energy when current flows
  through a conductor with resistance. The heat produced depends on the current, resistance,
  and time.
symbol: null
unit: null
equation: H = I²·R·t (Joule's law of heating)
examples:
  - "An electric kettle and toaster heat because current flows through resistance."
  - "A bulb filament glows white-hot from the heating effect of current."
key_experiments:
  - "Immerse a resistor in water, pass a current, and measure the temperature rise over time (heating ∝ I²R)."
common_misconceptions:
  - All electrical energy becomes heat (some becomes light, mechanical energy, etc.).
  - Heating effect is always undesirable (it's useful in heaters, fuses, and bulbs).
  - Thicker wires heat more (thicker wires have less resistance and heat less).
learning_objectives:
  - Explain the heating effect of electric current.
  - Calculate heat produced using H = I²Rt.
  - "Describe applications: electric heaters, fuses, bulbs."
  - Explain why wires heat up when current flows.
real_world_applications:
  - Electric heaters and irons.
  - Fuses and circuit breakers.
  - Incandescent light bulbs.
  - Electric welding.
