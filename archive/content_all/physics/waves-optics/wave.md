definition: A disturbance that transfers energy through a medium or through space,
  without permanently displacing the medium. Waves transfer energy and information
  from one place to another.

examples:
- Sound travels through air as a longitudinal wave.
- Light and radio travel as transverse electromagnetic waves and need no medium.
key_experiments:
- 'Slinky: a sideways flick makes a transverse pulse; a push-pull makes a longitudinal
  pulse.'
- Ripple tank experiments demonstrating reflection, refraction, and diffraction of
  water waves.
common_misconceptions:
- Waves carry matter (waves carry energy; the medium oscillates but does not travel
  with the wave).
- All waves need a medium (electromagnetic waves travel through vacuum).
- Waves always move in straight lines (waves can diffract and refract).
learning_objectives:
- Define wave as a disturbance that transfers energy.
- Distinguish between transverse and longitudinal waves.
- Identify wavelength, frequency, amplitude, and speed on a wave diagram.
- Relate wave speed, frequency, and wavelength using v = f·λ.

- Communication (radio, mobile phones, Wi-Fi).

- Seismology (earthquake waves).

## Notes

Waves are one of the most important mechanisms for transferring energy in the universe. From
sound to light to radio, understanding waves is essential for modern technology and for
understanding the physical world.
