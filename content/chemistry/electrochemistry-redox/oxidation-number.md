name: Oxidation Number
domain: chemistry

definition: >-
  A bookkeeping number assigned to an atom in a compound or ion that represents the charge the atom
  would have if all bonds to atoms of different electronegativity were ionic. Oxidation numbers track
  electron transfer in oxidation-reduction reactions.
symbol: null
unit: null
equation: null
examples:
  - "In H₂O the oxidation numbers are +1 for H and −2 for O."
  - "In MnO₄⁻, manganese has oxidation number +7."
key_experiments:
  - "Use visible redox titrations, such as permanganate, to confirm electron-transfer stoichiometry."
common_misconceptions:
  - Oxidation number is a real charge felt by the atom (it is a bookkeeping convention).
  - Atoms in a molecule must all have nonzero oxidation numbers (some atoms have oxidation number zero).
learning_objectives:
  - Assign oxidation numbers by applying the standard rules.
  - Use oxidation numbers to identify oxidation and reduction.
  - Balance redox equations using changes in oxidation number.
real_world_applications:
  - Naming inorganic compounds and balancing industrial redox processes.
  - Understanding oxidation states in corrosion and metallurgy.
