---
id: stemma:chem.catalyst
type: concept
name: Catalyst
domain: chemistry
status: draft
definition: >-
  A substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction by providing an alternative reaction
  pathway of lower activation energy, while itself being regenerated and not consumed overall. A
  catalyst does not alter the equilibrium position or product yield.
symbol: null
unit: null
equation: null
examples:
  - "Iron catalyzes the Haber synthesis of ammonia; platinum catalyzes the oxidation of ammonia to nitric oxide."
  - "Enzymes are biological catalysts that accelerate metabolic reactions at body temperature."
key_experiments:
  - "Add manganese dioxide to hydrogen peroxide and compare oxygen evolution rates with and without it."
common_misconceptions:
  - A catalyst is consumed by the reaction (it is recycled and not used up).
  - A catalyst changes the final equilibrium amounts (it only speeds the approach to equilibrium).
learning_objectives:
  - Explain how a catalyst lowers activation energy and increases reaction rate.
  - Distinguish homogeneous and heterogeneous catalysis.
  - Recognize the role of enzymes as selective biological catalysts.
real_world_applications:
  - Catalytic converters in vehicles reduce pollutant emissions.
  - Industrial catalysts enable economical production of ammonia, fuels, and plastics.
