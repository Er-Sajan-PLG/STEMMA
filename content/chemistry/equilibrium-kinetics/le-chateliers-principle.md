name: Le Chatelier's Principle
domain: chemistry
status: draft
definition: >-
  The principle stating that if a dynamic equilibrium is disturbed by changing a condition such as
  concentration, pressure, or temperature, the equilibrium shifts in the direction that tends to
  counteract that change and re-establish equilibrium.
symbol: null
unit: null
equation: null
examples:
  - "Increasing pressure shifts the ammonia synthesis equilibrium toward the side with fewer gas moles."
  - "Raising temperature favors the endothermic direction of a reversible reaction."
key_experiments:
  - "Add concentrated acid to a cobalt complex equilibrium and observe the color shift."
common_misconceptions:
  - Le Chatelier's principle predicts the new equilibrium constant (temperature is the only condition that changes K).
  - A catalyst shifts the equilibrium toward products (a catalyst speeds both directions equally).
learning_objectives:
  - Predict the direction of equilibrium shift under changes in concentration, pressure, and temperature.
  - Apply the principle to optimize reaction yield in industrial processes.
  - Recognize that a catalyst does not change the equilibrium position.
real_world_applications:
  - Optimizing the Haber and Contact industrial processes.
  - Controlling gas exchange and acid-base balance in the body.
