id: stemma:chem.alkane
type: concept
name: Alkane
domain: chemistry
status: draft
definition: >-
  A saturated hydrocarbon in which every carbon atom is bonded to others by single covalent bonds only, with the general formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ for acyclic alkanes. Alkanes are the simplest
  homologous series of organic compounds.
symbol: null
unit: null
equation: "C_n H_{2n+2}  (acyclic alkanes, n ≥ 1)"
examples:
  - "Methane (CH₄), ethane (C₂H₆), and propane (C₃H₈) are the first three alkanes."
  - "Octane (C₈H₁₈) is a key component of motor fuels."
key_experiments:
  - "Compare the reactivity of methane with bromine under light to show halogen substitution."
common_misconceptions:
  - Alkanes are highly reactive (their single bonds make them relatively unreactive).
  - All alkanes are gases (boiling point rises with chain length).
learning_objectives:
  - Write the general formula and names of simple alkanes.
  - Explain why alkanes are relatively unreactive.
  - Describe combustion and halogen substitution reactions of alkanes.
real_world_applications:
  - Natural gas (methane) and petroleum fractions used as fuels.
  - Waxes and paraffins used in candles and coatings.

historical:
  stated_by: "Recognition of the paraffin (alkane) series in 19th-century hydrocarbon chemistry"
  year: 1830
  where: "19th-century hydrocarbon chemistry"
  note: "The saturated hydrocarbons were recognised as a family (the paraffins) during the 19th century; the term 'paraffin' was coined by Carl von Reichenbach (c. 1830). The systematic 'alkane' nomenclature came later with the development of organic nomenclature, so no single discoverer is attributed."
