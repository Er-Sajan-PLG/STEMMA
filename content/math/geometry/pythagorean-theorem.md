name: Pythagorean Theorem
domain: mathematics
status: draft
definition: >-
  In a right triangle, the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of squares of the other two sides.
  This is one of the most fundamental theorems in geometry, connecting algebra and geometry.
symbol: a² + b² = c²
unit: null
equation: a² + b² = c²
examples:
  - "3-4-5 triangle: 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5²"
  - "Ladder problem: base 3m from wall, top 4m high → ladder = 5m"
  - "Diagonal of rectangle: w² + h² = d²"
common_misconceptions:
  - "Works for all triangles (only right triangles)"
  - "c is always the longest side (yes, hypotenuse is longest, but must identify which is c)"
  - "a² + b² = c² means a + b = c (false; √(a²+b²) ≠ a+b)"
learning_objectives:
  - State and apply the theorem
  - Identify right triangles using the converse (if a²+b²=c², then right angle)
  - Solve real-world problems (distance, height, diagonal)
  - "Extend to 3D: distance in space = √(x²+y²+z²)"
real_world_applications:
  - Construction (ensuring right angles)
  - Navigation (shortest distance)
  - Physics (vector magnitude)
  - Computer graphics (distance calculation)
key_experiments:
  - "Rope with 12 equally spaced knots: form 3-4-5 triangle for right angle"
