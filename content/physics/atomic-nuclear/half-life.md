id: stemma:phys.half-life
type: quantity
name: Half-Life
domain: physics
status: draft
definition: The time required for half the nuclei in a radioactive sample to decay. It is a
  statistically defined constant for a given nuclide and independent of the sample size.
symbol: T1/2
unit: second (s)
equation: N(t) = N0·(1/2)^(t/T1/2)
examples:
  - "Carbon-14 decays with a half-life of about 5730 years, used in dating organic remains."
  - "A short-lived medical isotope loses half its activity in a matter of minutes."
key_experiments:
  - "Record the count rate of a radioactive source at successive times and show it halves each half-life."
common_misconceptions:
  - "Half-life is a statistical average, not a time by which every individual nucleus has decayed."
  - "Heating, pressure, or chemical state do not change a nuclide's half-life."
learning_objectives:
  - "Apply the half-life relation to compute remaining nuclei or activity."
  - "Use half-life to estimate the age of a sample via radiometric dating."
real_world_applications:
  - "Carbon-14 and potassium-argon dating in archaeology and geology."
  - "Planning radiation therapy and managing radioactive waste decay."
