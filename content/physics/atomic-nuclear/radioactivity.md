name: Radioactivity
domain: physics
status: draft
definition: The spontaneous emission of radiation from an unstable atomic nucleus.
  The three main types are alpha particles, beta particles, and gamma rays.
symbol: null
unit: null
equation: null
examples:
- Carbon-14 dating is used on ancient organic remains; radioactive tracers image organs
  in medicine.
- Smoke detectors contain a small alpha-emitting source.
key_experiments:
- Use a Geiger counter to detect background radiation and compare shielding/absorption
  of different sources.
- Detecting radiation using a Geiger-Muller tube.
common_misconceptions:
- Radioactivity is always man-made (it occurs naturally).
- All radiation is harmful (we are exposed to natural background radiation daily).
- Radioactive materials glow (they are invisible; only their effects can be detected).
learning_objectives:
- Describe radioactivity as spontaneous nuclear decay.
- Distinguish between alpha, beta, and gamma radiation.
- Explain half-life and calculate remaining amounts.
- Describe uses and hazards of radiation.
real_world_applications:
- Nuclear power.
- Medical imaging and cancer treatment.
- Carbon dating.
- Sterilization of medical equipment.
