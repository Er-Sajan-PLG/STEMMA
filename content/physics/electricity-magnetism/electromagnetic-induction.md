name: Electromagnetic Induction
domain: physics
status: draft
definition: >-
  The production of an electromotive force (voltage) across a conductor when it is exposed to
  a changing magnetic field. This is the principle behind electric generators.
symbol: null
unit: null
equation: ε = -dΦ/dt (Faraday's law)
examples:
  - "A dynamo generates electricity as a coil rotates in a magnetic field."
  - "Induction cooktops heat pans through induced (eddy) currents."
key_experiments:
  - "Galvanometer + magnet-in-coil: relative motion induces current, reversing with direction (Faraday's law)."
common_misconceptions:
  - A stationary magnet near a coil produces current (the magnetic field must be changing).
  - Induced voltage depends on the magnet's strength alone (it depends on the rate of change of flux).
learning_objectives:
  - Explain electromagnetic induction.
  - Describe Faraday's law of induction.
  - Explain how generators produce electricity.
  - Apply Lenz's law to determine the direction of induced current.
real_world_applications:
  - Electric generators.
  - Transformers.
  - Induction cooktops.
  - Wireless charging.
