id: stemma:phys.magnetic-force
type: quantity
name: Magnetic Force
domain: physics
status: draft
definition: The force exerted by a magnetic field on a moving charge or on a current-carrying
  conductor. It is perpendicular to both the velocity of the charge and the magnetic field, so
  it changes the direction of motion but not the speed of a free charge.
symbol: F
unit: newton (N)
equation: F = q·v×B for a charge q moving at velocity v in field B
examples:
  - "A charged particle entering a uniform magnetic field at right angles moves in a circular path."
  - "A current-carrying wire in a magnetic field experiences a deflecting force."
key_experiments:
  - "Pass a current through a wire suspended between magnet poles and observe perpendicular deflection."
common_misconceptions:
  - "A stationary charge feels no magnetic force; only moving charges or currents experience it."
  - "The magnetic force does no work on a free charge because it is always perpendicular to velocity."
learning_objectives:
  - "Determine the direction of magnetic force using the right-hand rule."
  - "Explain why a magnetic field alone cannot change a charge's kinetic energy."
real_world_applications:
  - "Particle accelerators and mass spectrometers steer beams with magnetic fields."
  - "Electric motors convert magnetic force into rotational motion."
