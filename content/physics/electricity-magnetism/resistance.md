type: quantity
name: Electrical Resistance
domain: physics
status: draft
definition: >-
  The opposition to the flow of electric current in a conductor. Resistance converts electrical
  energy to thermal energy.
symbol: R
unit: ohm (Ω)
equation: R = V / I
examples:
  - "A thin wire resists more than a thick one; metal conducts far better than wood."
  - "Heater elements are deliberately high-resistance so they heat up."
key_experiments:
  - "Vary the voltage across a fixed resistor and plot current against voltage — straight line at constant temperature."
common_misconceptions:
  - Resistance is the same as voltage (resistance is opposition; voltage is potential difference).
  - Thicker wires always have less resistance (all else equal, yes — but length and material also matter).
  - Resistance is always undesirable (resistance enables heating, lighting, and control).
learning_objectives:
  - Define resistance as opposition to current.
  - Calculate resistance from voltage and current.
  - Explain factors affecting resistance (material, length, cross-sectional area, temperature).
  - Use the resistance formula R = ρL/A.
real_world_applications:
  - Heating elements (toasters, heaters).
  - Resistors in electronic circuits.
  - Fuses.
