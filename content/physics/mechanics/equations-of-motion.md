definition: >-
  The set of kinematic equations relating displacement, initial velocity, uniform
  acceleration, and time for motion in a straight line under constant acceleration:
  v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², v² = u² + 2as, and s = ½(u + v)t. They apply only when
  acceleration is constant.
symbol: v, u, a, s, t

equation: v = u + at;  s = ut + ½at²;  v² = u² + 2as;  s = ½(u+v)t
examples:
  - "A ball dropped from rest falls with v = g·t after t seconds."
  - "Estimating a car's braking distance from its speed and deceleration using v² = u² + 2as."
key_experiments:
  - "Braking/accelerating cart on a linear track: measure times and distances to verify v = u + at."
common_misconceptions:
  - Equations of motion apply whenever an object moves (they are valid only for uniform, constant acceleration).
  - u and v are interchangeable (u is the initial velocity and v is the final velocity; swapping them changes the answer).
  - The equations work for any motion including circular motion (they describe straight-line kinematics only).

## Notes

The equations of motion unify the kinematics quantities. Given constant acceleration,
any one quantity can be found from the others — this is the mathematical core of the
study of motion.
