id: stemma:phys.doppler-effect
type: concept
name: Doppler Effect
domain: physics
status: draft
definition: The apparent change in the observed frequency of a wave due to relative motion
  between the wave source and the observer. The observed frequency is higher as the source
  approaches and lower as it recedes.
symbol: null
unit: null
equation: f_obs = f · (v ± v_o)/(v ∓ v_s) for a wave of speed v
examples:
  - "The pitch of an ambulance siren rises as it approaches and falls as it passes."
  - "A police radar measures a car's speed from the frequency shift of a reflected wave."
key_experiments:
  - "Move a sound source past a stationary microphone and record the frequency jump at closest approach."
common_misconceptions:
  - "The Doppler shift is a change in observed frequency, not a change in the source's emitted frequency."
  - "The effect occurs for any wave, including light, as well as for sound."
learning_objectives:
  - "Compute the observed frequency given source and observer velocities for a moving medium."
  - "Distinguish the Doppler effect for sound (medium-dependent) from that for light (relativistic)."
real_world_applications:
  - "Weather radar and Doppler ultrasound imaging."
  - "Astronomical red- and blue-shift measurements of distant stars and galaxies."
